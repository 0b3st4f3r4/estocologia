# Opinion — Case 04: new checkout

## Pre-registered metric
The plan approved before the test defined as the success metric the 7-day conversion of all those drawn. The cuts and the other windows were explored afterwards.

## Result on the pre-registered metric (3000 against 3000)
- 7-day conversion: 8.20% in the new against 8.33% in the current. Difference of −0.13 p.p., 95% CI from −1.53 to +1.26 p.p., p = 0.85.
- The exploratory windows point even to worse: 14 days, 11.7% against 12.5% (p = 0.32); 30 days, 14.5% against 15.9% (p = 0.14).

## The highlighted segment
- The cut "computer, up to 35 years old, customer who has already bought" is reproduced: 13.0% against 5.9%, p = 0.007.
- It is one of the 8 crossings of device × age band × customer type, and the age cut at 35 is also a free choice. Adding up windows, cuts and cutoffs, the effective number of comparisons is much larger than 8. Bonferroni over the 8 cells alone already takes p to 0.058.
- Another cell, "mobile, over 35, customer who has already bought" (n = 1451), shows the new checkout as worse: 6.0% against 9.1%, p = 0.027. The pattern of "significant" cells in opposite directions is what is expected by chance when one digs through cuts.
- Besides, the decision is to switch for all users. Even if the effect in the segment were real, it would not support the general switch: the mean effect is null and the CI excludes gains above ~1.3 p.p.

## Conclusion
The test, analysed as planned, shows no increase in conversion. The segment is the result of a post hoc search and does not survive correction for multiplicity. If there is interest in the segment, it should be tested in a new confirmatory experiment, with a pre-registered hypothesis.
