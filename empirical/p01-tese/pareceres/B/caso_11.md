# Opinion — Case 11: diesel additive

## Results
- Consumption: 30.92 L/100 km with additive against 36.01 without, a difference of −5.09 L/100 km (−14.1%). By Student's t test, p = 0.0067 (Welch p = 0.009). The 95% CI of the difference goes from −8.3 to −1.9 L/100 km, that is, from 5% to 23% of savings.
- Exact randomization test (252 possible allocations): p = 0.008. The separation is complete: the 5 trucks with additive consume less than the 5 without (Mann-Whitney U = 0).
- The kilometers driven are similar (10,732 against 10,187 km).

## Why this does not authorize budgeting 14% of savings for 800 trucks
1. **Imprecision**: the CI goes from 5% to 23%. The 14% are a point estimate with 10 trucks, and budgeting at the central value ignores that 5% is equally compatible with the data. The additive may not pay for itself in that scenario.
2. **Plausibility and the "winner's curse"**: a saving of 14% coming from an additive is very large for the physics of a diesel engine. Large and "significant" effects coming from tiny samples tend to be overestimated. The client himself says that consumption varies a lot from truck to truck and from month to month. With n = 5 per group, one or two trucks in an atypical month (C06, with 26.65 L/100 km, is the lowest of all) move the mean a lot.
3. **Duration and design**: it was a single month, with no measurement of the consumption of the same trucks before the test (which would allow a paired comparison or adjustment by the baseline), and the draw was made by the supplier, an interested party. We do not know whether the effect holds on other routes, seasons and drivers.
4. **Generalization**: 10 trucks of the same model and year on similar routes do not represent a fleet of 800.

## Recommendation
Do a larger test before the contract: dozens of trucks, several months, a draw made by the carrier itself, comparison with the previous consumption of each truck (or a crossover design), and decision by the lower limit of the CI of the savings against the cost of the additive.
